# Press Release Stage

Use this stage to force customer-first clarity through a lightweight
product-announcement narrative, not a corporate press-release exercise.

## Goal

Draft a future announcement that would be honest if the concept shipped and
customers loved it. It should explain:

- who the product is for
- what painful problem changed
- why now
- what outcome is now possible
- why that outcome beats the status quo
- what the smallest credible V1 includes and excludes

## Coaching Path

Ask one question at a time. Resolve earlier weak dependencies before moving
deeper.

1. Who exactly is the first user/customer or segment?
2. What recurring situation creates the pain?
3. What do they do today instead?
4. Why is now the right time to solve this?
5. What would change after launch that they would notice?
6. What claim can be made without exaggeration?
7. What narrower V1 would make the claim more credible?
8. What should the announcement deliberately not promise?

## Draft Shape

Use natural prose, but preserve this intent:

```markdown
# [Concept name] helps [specific customer] [changed outcome]

[Team/company] is exploring [concept], a [plain-language category] for
[specific customer] who struggle with [specific problem].

Today, [customer] relies on [status quo], which causes [stakes].

The proposed V1 changes this by [changed outcome] through [core mechanism].

The first version focuses on [first segment / wedge] and deliberately excludes
[non-goals].
```

## Pressure Tests

Challenge the draft when:

- the headline describes a feature instead of an outcome
- the customer is "everyone", "teams", or "users" without specificity
- the problem is an internal desire rather than customer pain
- the promise depends on unverified technical or market assumptions
- the release sounds impressive but not useful
- the scope is too broad for the available evidence

Useful prompts:

- "What would the customer stop doing because this exists?"
- "What would make them distrust or ignore this?"
- "What is the smallest version that still justifies the announcement?"
- "If we removed the technology label, would the problem still matter?"
- "Which sentence would be embarrassing if we had to prove it tomorrow?"

## Exit Criteria

Move forward only when the announcement has:

- specific customer or first segment
- concrete problem and status quo
- why-now
- changed outcome
- credible initial scope
- visible non-goals
