# Internal FAQ Stage

Use this stage to test whether the organization can responsibly build, launch,
operate, support, and maintain the concept.

## Start With V1

Do not evaluate feasibility against a vague vision. First lock the smallest
credible V1: first customer/team/segment, included workflow, excluded scope,
source systems, trust model, and success signal.

## Required Question Areas

Adapt to the concept, but cover relevant areas:

- What is the hardest product risk?
- What is the hardest technical risk?
- What assumptions must be true?
- What data, integrations, permissions, or source systems are required?
- What can fail, and what will users see when it fails?
- What must be measured before broader rollout?
- Who builds, operates, supports, and maintains it?
- What support burden or operational workflow does this create?
- What costs, capacity constraints, or performance limits matter?
- What alternatives were rejected, and why?
- What should be deferred from V1?

## Concept-Type Calibration

For commercial products, include buyer, willingness to pay, packaging, pricing,
and business model assumptions.

For internal tools, replace commercial questions with adoption cost, operating
cost, workflow disruption, team outcome, and maintenance ownership.

For compliance needs, focus on risk reduction, auditability, deadlines,
controls, and required evidence.

For platform capabilities, focus on reusability, consuming teams, API/contracts,
operational ownership, and migration burden.

## Conditional Risk Areas

Ask accessibility, legal, security, privacy, compliance, and abuse questions
when the concept touches regulated domains, sensitive data, user-facing UI,
permissions, automation, generated content, minors, employment, finance,
healthcare, education, or other high-risk contexts.

## Reframing Moves

- Broad platform -> narrow workflow with one high-stakes job.
- Opaque automation -> evidence-backed assistant with human confirmation.
- Many integrations -> one or two source systems with strong traceability.
- Big launch -> limited pilot with measured trust and adoption signals.
- Autonomous recommendation -> ranked attention cue with source links.

## Exit Criteria

Move to verdict only when internal objections have produced:

- credible V1 scope
- known ownership and operational responsibilities
- clear assumptions
- dependency list
- measurement needs
- deferred scope
- major risks and constraints
